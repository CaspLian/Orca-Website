Orca Diets

General Prey

  • Fish: Fish-eating orcas prey on around 30 species of fish. Some populations in the Norwegian and Greenland sea specialize in herring and follow that fish's autumnal migration to the Norwegian coast.
  • Mammals and birds: They are recorded to prey on other cetacean species, usually smaller dolphins and porpoises such as common dolphins, bottlenose dolphins, Pacific white-sided dolphins, dusky dolphins, harbour porpoises and Dall's porpoises.

Diet based on location

Orcas are apex predators, meaning that they themselves have no natural predators. They are sometimes called "wolves of the sea", because they hunt in groups like wolf packs. Orcas hunt varied prey including fish, cephalopods, mammals, seabirds, and sea turtles. Different populations or ecotypes may specialize, and some can have a dramatic impact on prey species. However, whales in tropical areas appear to have more generalized diets due to lower food productivity. Orcas spend most of their time at shallow depths, but occasionally dive several hundred metres depending on their prey

North Pacific: Resident killer whales eat primarily salmon, while transient killer whales eat small marine mammals like seals and porpoises. Offshore killer whales eat a variety of fish and sleeper sharks.

North Atlantic: Type 1 killer whales eat seals and schooling fish like herring and mackerel, while Type 2 killer whales eat cetaceans like dolphins, porpoises, and baleen whales.

Hawaiian waters: Killer whales in Hawaiian waters eat a varied diet that includes humpback whales, dolphins, octopuses, and squids.

New Zealand:Killer whales in New Zealand waters eat sharks and rays.
